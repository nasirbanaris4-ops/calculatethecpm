Average CTV and OTT CPM: Benchmarks and How to Check Yours

Published November 5, 2026

A good CTV or OTT CPM is one that still meets your result goal after you look past the blended average. Several 2026 benchmarks put the blended US CTV CPM near $25 to $26, but individual buys range from about $15 on the open exchange to $65 or more for premium and live-sports placements.

CPM (cost per mille) is the price of 1,000 ad impressions: spend ÷ impressions × 1,000. To check a specific buy, use our CPM calculator, then compare it against your own past CTV campaigns.

CTV/OTT CPM: key takeaways

  • A blended average CTV CPM hides a wide spread. Ask for CPM by inventory tier, not one blended figure.
  • CTV is TV-screen viewing. OTT is the broader streaming category across all devices; CTV sits inside it.
  • Premium, live-sports and audience-targeted inventory costs more than open-exchange or FAST inventory.
  • A buyer's gross CPM and a publisher's net CPM are not the same number.

What do CTV and OTT mean?

IAB UK defines CTV as a subset of OTT: OTT covers all internet-delivered streaming, on any device, while CTV refers specifically to that streaming watched on a television screen. Most OTT viewing in the US happens on a CTV device, so the two terms are often used together, but a mobile-only streaming view is OTT and not CTV.

None of these is an official, audited rate card. Most converge on a blended figure near $25–$26, but the full range runs from about $5 (publisher net, open programmatic) to $85 (premium live sports). Treat any single number as a starting point, not a target.

Why the gap is so wide

A blended average mixes very different inventory. A buy that averages $26 can contain line items that differ by 4x.

  • Inventory tier: open exchange and FAST channels price lowest; programmatic guaranteed sits in the middle; direct-sold premium and live sports price highest.
  • Genre: live sports and news command the highest publisher CPMs, while movies and faith content run lower (OTT Engine).
  • Buy-side vs sell-side: a buyer's gross CPM is not the publisher's net CPM. Only about 45–55% of an advertiser's CTV dollar reaches the publisher as working media.
  • Region: non-US markets often price at 0.6 to 0.9x the US baseline.

How to judge your own CTV or OTT CPM

  1. Ask for CPM by tier. A blended number hides the mix. Request open exchange, programmatic guaranteed and premium direct as separate line items.
  2. Match genre to genre. Compare a sports buy with other sports benchmarks, not with a blended average that includes cheaper content.
  3. Confirm gross or net. A buyer's quote and a publisher's net CPM are different numbers. Ask which one you are looking at.
  4. Check inventory quality. A cheap CPM on unverified open-exchange supply can cost more per real view once fraud and non-human traffic are excluded.
  5. Compare with your own history. Your best benchmark is your own past CTV campaigns on the same platform, tier and region. For the general version of this approach, see What Is a Good CPM?

Frequently asked questions

1. What is a good CTV CPM?

There is no single good CTV CPM. Several 2026 sources put the blended US average near $25 to $26, with open-exchange inventory as low as $15 and premium or live-sports placements reaching $65 or more. Judge your CPM against your own results, not a blended industry number.

2. What is the difference between CTV and OTT?

CTV (connected TV) is TV-screen viewing through an internet-connected device. OTT (over-the-top) is broader: it covers streaming on any device, including mobile, desktop and tablets, not only the TV screen. IAB UK, the industry trade body, defines CTV as a subset of OTT.

3. Why do CTV CPM benchmarks vary so much?

Because they measure different things: inventory tier (open exchange versus premium direct), genre (news and live sports cost more than movies), platform, region, and whether the figure is a buyer's gross CPM or a publisher's net CPM after fees. A single blended average can hide a 4x spread between tiers.

4. Is CTV CPM higher than CPM on YouTube or social?

Usually yes. CTV ads are typically full-screen and non-skippable with near-complete viewability, which commands a premium over feed-based video. Compare CTV only with other full-screen, TV-like formats, not with skippable in-feed ads.
