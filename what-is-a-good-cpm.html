What Is a Good CPM? Benchmarks and How to Check Yours

Published November 3, 2026 · Last updated November 3, 2026

A good CPM is one that delivers results at or below your target cost per result. There is no single universal number: most published 2026 Meta medians in the sources below fall between about $14 and $28, so compare your CPM with your own history first.

CPM (cost per mille) is the price of 1,000 ad impressions: spend ÷ impressions × 1,000. To check your own number, use our CPM calculator, then compare cost per result, not just CPM.

Key takeaways

  • A cheap CPM is not automatically good, and an expensive CPM is not automatically bad.
  • Published benchmarks disagree because their samples, countries, and dates differ.
  • Your best benchmark is your own past campaigns on the same platform and goal.
  • Judge CPM together with cost per result.

Even for DTC brands the sources split: Biddy reports a $16.02 median for DTC, while MHI reports $28.28 for its DTC sample.

The overall gap is large: $6.59 to $28.28. These are third-party figures, not official Meta benchmarks. Treat them as context, not targets.

One source even labels roughly $8 to $12 as good (ContentStudio), which is below the medians in the other samples. That is another sign that "good" depends on who is measuring.

What changes your CPM?

Meta says ad delivery depends on an auction. A total value score combines your bid, the estimated action rate, and ad quality, and the highest bid does not always win.

That means CPM reflects more than your budget:

  • Country and audience: the same ad costs very different amounts in different markets.
  • Season: Biddy, an ad agency, reported its median rising from $15.96 in June to $20.10 in August 2026.
  • Creative and relevance: ads people respond to can win auctions more cheaply.
  • Campaign goal: reach campaigns and conversion campaigns are not priced alike.

How do you check if your CPM is good?

  1. Set a target. Decide the most you can pay per result (lead, sale, or click).
  2. Calculate CPM. Spend ÷ impressions × 1,000. Our Excel and Google Sheets guide shows the formulas.
  3. Calculate cost per result. Spend ÷ results.
  4. Compare. If cost per result is at or below target, your CPM is good enough for that campaign.
  5. Compare with yourself. Use the same platform, goal, country, and a similar date range.

What about Google Display, YouTube, and TikTok?

Google describes CPM bidding on the Display Network as paying per thousand views. Google Display, YouTube, and TikTok each price impressions differently, so look for current figures that state their method before you compare them with Meta.

Frequently asked questions about a good CPM

1. What is a good CPM for Facebook ads?

There is no single good CPM. In the 2026 third-party samples above, Meta medians ranged from about $14 to about $28. Judge yours by whether your cost per result stays at or below your target.

2. Is a low CPM always good?

No. A low CPM only means impressions are cheap. In the example above, Campaign C has the lowest CPM ($5.00) but the highest cost per result ($62.50).

3. Why is my CPM higher than a benchmark?

Benchmarks come from different countries, industries, time periods, and campaign goals. Season, audience, creative, and auction competition also change CPM. A higher CPM is a reason to check results, not proof of a problem.

4. Which CPM benchmark should I trust?

Prefer sources that state their sample size, time period, region, and campaign goal. None of the sources above is an official platform benchmark, so use them as context and trust your own account history most.
