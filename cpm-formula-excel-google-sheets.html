CPM Formula in Excel and Google Sheets (With Free Template)

Published November 2, 2026

To calculate CPM in Excel or Google Sheets, divide spend by impressions and multiply by 1,000. If spend is in B2 and impressions are in C2, enter =B2/C2*1000. Example: $600 spent on 120,000 impressions gives a CPM of $5.00.

Key takeaways

  • CPM formula: =B2/C2*1000 (spend ÷ impressions × 1,000).
  • Combined CPM: add spend and impressions first, then calculate. Never average row CPMs.
  • Zero impressions break the formula. Use the guarded version below.
  • Do not mix currencies or reporting periods in one total.

Step 1: Set up your spreadsheet

Create four columns:

Column Header Content
A Campaign Campaign name
B Spend (USD) Cost as a number
C Impressions Whole number
D CPM (USD) Formula

Use these hypothetical campaigns (USD, same reporting period):

Campaign Spend (USD) Impressions
Campaign A 600 120,000
Campaign B 400 50,000
Campaign C 250 100,000

Impressions count ad displays. They do not count unique people, clicks, or page views.

Step 2: Enter the CPM formula

In D2, type:

=B2/C2*1000

Press Enter. The result is 5. Format it as currency to show $5.00.

Copy D2 down to D3 and D4. Expected results:

Campaign CPM
Campaign A $5.00
Campaign B $8.00
Campaign C $2.50

The same formula works in Excel and Google Sheets. Currency formatting changes how the number looks. It does not convert currency.

Step 3: Handle blank cells and zero impressions

CPM cannot be calculated when impressions are zero. Microsoft explains that dividing by zero or a blank cell causes a #DIV/0! error.

Use this guarded formula in D2:

=IF(OR(B2="",C2=""),"",IF(OR(B2<0,C2<=0),"Check inputs",B2/C2*1000))

What it does:

  • Leaves the cell blank if an input is missing.
  • Shows "Check inputs" for negative spend or zero impressions.
  • Calculates CPM in all other cases.

Avoid IFERROR(...,0). A CPM of zero looks like a real result. Google explains how the IF function works.

Tip: If your sheet uses semicolons, replace the commas between arguments with semicolons. Locale settings decide this.

Step 4: Calculate combined CPM correctly

Add a Totals row in row 5:

Cell Formula
B5 =SUM(B2:B4)
C5 =SUM(C2:C4)
D5 =B5/C5*1000

Total spend is $1,250. Total impressions are 270,000. Combined CPM is $1,250 ÷ 270,000 × 1,000 = $4.63.

Do not use =AVERAGE(D2:D4). It returns about $5.17 because it treats every campaign equally, even though their impressions differ. Combined CPM gives every impression equal weight.

Step 5: Keep currencies and dates consistent

  • Do not add USD, GBP, and EUR into one total.
  • Report each currency separately, or convert using a documented rate and date.
  • Use the same date range for every row.
  • Label whether CPM is media-only or includes fees.
  • Do not add a campaign total and its ad-group rows together. That counts the same spend twice.

Step 6: Check your results before sharing

  1. Verify one row by hand.
  2. Confirm copied formulas point to the right cells.
  3. Check that impressions are numbers, not text.
  4. Compare unusual results with the original platform export.

CPM measures delivery cost, not results. Campaign C has the lowest CPM here, but that does not show which campaign produced the best leads or sales. See our CPM vs CPC vs CPA guide for the difference.

Frequently asked questions

1. What is the CPM formula in Excel?

Use =B2/C2*1000, where B2 is spend and C2 is impressions. The result is cost per 1,000 impressions. Cross-check with our CPM calculator.

2. Is the CPM formula the same in Google Sheets?

Yes. The basic formula is identical. Only the argument separator (comma or semicolon) in longer formulas may differ by locale.

3. Should I average CPM values in a spreadsheet?

No. For combined CPM, divide total spend by total impressions and multiply by 1,000. A simple average is correct only when every row has the same impressions.

4. Can I calculate eCPM with this sheet?

Yes, for publisher reports. Replace spend with ad revenue and label the result eCPM. Keep ad impressions as the denominator. Use our eCPM calculator for a quick check. Never mix revenue rows with advertiser-spend rows in one total.
