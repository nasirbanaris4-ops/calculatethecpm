CPM vs CPC vs CPA: which metric should you use?

By CalculateTheCPM. Reviewed October 28, 2026.

The quick answer

CPM measures spend per 1,000 ad impressions, CPC measures spend per click, and CPA measures spend per recorded acquisition or action. Use CPM to understand delivery cost, CPC to understand traffic cost, and CPA to understand conversion cost. They describe different stages of the same campaign.

Three formulas, one campaign

Suppose an illustrative campaign spends $600, delivers 120,000 impressions, generates 1,200 clicks and records 30 purchases. These figures are a teaching example, not market benchmarks.

Cost metrics for the same $600 campaign
MetricFormulaResult
CPMSpend ÷ impressions × 1,000$600 ÷ 120,000 × 1,000 = $5
CPCSpend ÷ clicks$600 ÷ 1,200 = $0.50
CPASpend ÷ acquisitions$600 ÷ 30 = $20

All three results can be correct at once. To calculate the CPM, enter spend and impressions in our CPM calculator. Use the CPC calculator for spend and click totals. Always use inputs from the same campaign and reporting period.

When CPM is useful

CPM helps you estimate how much ad delivery a budget can buy. At a hypothetical $5 CPM, a $1,000 budget buys 200,000 impressions if the rate stays constant: $1,000 ÷ $5 × 1,000. Actual auctions can change the rate as delivery progresses.

An impression is an ad display, not a unique person. Repeated displays can increase impressions without increasing reach by the same amount. Also check the denominator: viewable CPM uses viewable impressions, which should not be mixed with a CPM calculated from all served impressions. Google's viewable-impression bidding guidance explains that distinction.

When CPC is useful

CPC describes the cost of generating a click. It is helpful when comparing traffic efficiency, but the clicks must have matching definitions. A platform's all-clicks total can include interactions other than visits to your website. Check whether you are comparing link clicks, outbound clicks or another reported event.

A lower CPC does not establish that the visitors are more valuable. Compare landing-page engagement and recorded conversions before moving budget.

When CPA is useful

CPA connects spend to an outcome such as a sale, qualified lead or signup. Specify the action before comparing campaigns. A $10 newsletter signup and a $20 purchase are different outcomes, even though both can appear under a conversion label.

Recorded CPA also depends on attribution windows, conversion tracking and reporting delays. A target CPA is a bidding objective; it is not a promise that every conversion will cost that amount. CPA alone does not establish profit: consider margins, refunds and repeat purchases as appropriate for your business.

How CTR connects CPM and CPC

For matching spend, impressions and click totals, CPC = CPM ÷ (10 × CTR percentage). In the example, CTR is 1%: 1,200 ÷ 120,000 × 100. Therefore $5 ÷ (10 × 1) gives a $0.50 CPC.

If CTR increases to 2% while CPM remains $5, the implied CPC becomes $0.25. This is an arithmetic scenario, not a prediction that changing a creative will hold CPM constant. Try the CPM to CPC calculator and CTR calculator with your own totals.

Compare US, UK and European campaigns fairly

The formulas work in dollars, pounds or euros. For example, £600 divided by 120,000 impressions gives £5 CPM; €600 gives €5 CPM. These examples do not imply equivalent purchasing power or equal market costs.

Before comparing regions, align currencies using a consistent exchange-rate method, reporting dates, objectives, placements and attribution rules. Analyze European countries individually where possible. A blended regional CPM can shift simply because the mix of countries changed.

A practical decision sequence

  1. Check that impression, click and conversion tracking use consistent definitions.
  2. Calculate CPM to inspect delivery cost.
  3. Check CTR and CPC to see how delivery turns into traffic.
  4. Check conversion rate and CPA to see how traffic turns into the selected outcome.
  5. Compare revenue and costs before deciding whether to scale the campaign.

Common questions

Is CPM better than CPC?

Neither is universally better. CPM answers a delivery-cost question and CPC answers a click-cost question. Choose the metric that matches the decision and evaluate the downstream outcome.

Can a low CPM produce a high CPA?

Yes. Cheap impressions can still produce few clicks or conversions. Calculate each stage before assuming that inexpensive delivery means inexpensive customer acquisition.

What if a campaign has no conversions?

CPA cannot be calculated by dividing by zero. Report spend and zero recorded conversions, then check tracking, reporting delay and campaign performance. Do not display a zero CPA.
