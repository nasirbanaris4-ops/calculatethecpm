What is CTR?

CTR (click-through rate) is clicks divided by impressions, multiplied by 100. Enter 2 for a 2% CTR when solving for clicks or impressions.

CTR formula and example

CTR (%) = (Clicks ÷ Impressions) × 100

Worked example

An ad receives 500 clicks from 25,000 impressions. CTR is (500 ÷ 25,000) × 100 = 2%. At that rate, 50,000 impressions would generate an estimated 1,000 clicks.

These figures illustrate the arithmetic. They are not market averages or guaranteed campaign results. Volume forecasts can contain fractions; actual clicks and impressions are whole counts.

Use the right click definition

Compare CTR using consistent click definitions, placements, and date ranges. Link CTR and all-click CTR can differ. A higher CTR can indicate stronger engagement, but it does not establish that visitors convert or that a campaign is profitable.

Frequently asked questions

Should I enter 2 or 0.02 for 2%?

Enter 2. This calculator takes CTR as a percentage and applies the division by 100 when calculating volume.

How do I calculate clicks from CTR?

Multiply impressions by CTR percentage and divide by 100. For example, 25,000 impressions at 2% give 500 clicks.

Is a high CTR always better?

Not necessarily. Evaluate conversion quality, acquisition costs, and revenue to understand whether clicks create value.

Can I use this calculator in the US, UK, and Europe?

Yes. CTR is a percentage, so the formula does not depend on currency or country. Use consistent click and impression definitions.
