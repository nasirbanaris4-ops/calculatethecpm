What is CPA?

CPA (cost per acquisition) is ad spend divided by the number of acquisitions. It shows the average amount you pay for each completed conversion, such as a sale or a signup.

CPA formula and example

CPA = Ad spend ÷ Acquisitions

Worked example

A campaign spends $2,000 and generates 40 acquisitions. CPA is $2,000 ÷ 40 = $50. At the same CPA, a $5,000 budget is expected to produce about 100 acquisitions.

These figures illustrate the arithmetic. They are not market averages or guaranteed campaign results. Budget forecasts can contain fractions; actual acquisitions are whole counts.

What counts as an acquisition?

An acquisition is whatever you define as a completed conversion: a purchase, a signed-up lead, an app install, or a new subscriber. Each ad platform and CRM counts this differently, and some apply attribution windows that change the count after the fact. Use the acquisition number from your own report, and compare CPA only between campaigns that define acquisition the same way.

CPA vs CPL vs CPC

These metrics price different stages of a funnel. CPC prices a single click. CPL prices a lead, an earlier-stage action like a form fill. CPA prices a completed acquisition, typically a sale or paying customer, and is usually the highest of the three for the same funnel.

To compare click costs, use our CPC calculator. To check return on the spend behind your acquisitions, use our ROAS calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate CPA?

Divide ad spend by the number of acquisitions. For example, $2,000 spent that produces 40 acquisitions gives a CPA of $2,000 ÷ 40 = $50.

What counts as an acquisition?

An acquisition is any action you count as a successful conversion: a sale, a signed-up lead, an app install, or a subscription. Platforms track this differently, so use the acquisition count from your own ad platform or CRM report.

What is the difference between CPA and CPL?

CPA (cost per acquisition) is usually the cost of a completed sale or paying customer. CPL (cost per lead) is the cost of an earlier-stage action, like a form fill, before that lead becomes a customer. CPA is typically higher than CPL for the same funnel.

What is a good CPA?

There is no single good CPA. A good CPA is one that stays at or below what a customer is worth to you (such as average order value or lifetime value), after accounting for margin. Compare your CPA against your own past campaigns on the same platform and goal rather than a generic benchmark.
