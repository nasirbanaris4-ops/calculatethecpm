What is CPM?

CPM (cost per mille) is the advertising cost per 1,000 ad impressions. Calculate CPM by dividing ad spend by impressions and multiplying by 1,000.

See what your campaign costs per 1,000 impressions

CPM formula and example, with the formula shown below.

CPM = (Ad spend ÷ Ad impressions) × 1,000

Worked example

A campaign spending $800 on 160,000 ad impressions has a CPM of ($800 ÷ 160,000) × 1,000 = $5. At the same CPM, a $1,000 budget buys 200,000 impressions.

These figures illustrate the arithmetic. They are not market averages or guaranteed campaign results. Volume forecasts can contain fractions; actual clicks and impressions are whole counts.

The three CPM calculations

  • Find CPM: CPM = (Ad spend ÷ Impressions) × 1,000. Use this when you know your spend and impressions.
  • Find ad spend: Ad spend = (CPM × Impressions) ÷ 1,000. Use this when planning a budget at a target CPM.
  • Find impressions: Impressions = (Ad spend ÷ CPM) × 1,000. Use this when setting a reach goal.

How to interpret your CPM

CPM measures the cost of delivering ads, not the cost of acquiring customers. Compare campaigns with similar objectives, placements, audiences, and reporting periods. Use CTR and CPC to assess traffic, then ROAS to assess attributed revenue.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good CPM?

There is no universal good CPM. Evaluate it against comparable campaigns and your conversion results. A low CPM can still deliver poor value if the audience does not respond.

Are impressions the same as people reached?

No. Impressions count ad displays, while reach counts people or accounts. One person can generate multiple impressions.

How do I calculate ad spend from CPM?

Multiply CPM by impressions and divide by 1,000. For example, $5 CPM and 200,000 impressions imply $1,000 in ad spend.

How do I calculate impressions from CPM?

Divide ad spend by CPM, then multiply by 1,000. For example, $1,000 in ad spend at a $5 CPM gives 200,000 impressions.

Can I use this calculator in any country?
