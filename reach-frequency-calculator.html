What are reach and frequency?

Reach is the number of unique people who saw your ad at least once. Frequency is the average number of times each person saw it. Impressions divided by reach gives average frequency.

Reach and frequency formula and example

Frequency = Impressions ÷ Reach

Worked example

A campaign delivers 500,000 impressions to a reach of 150,000 unique people. Average frequency is 500,000 ÷ 150,000 = 3.3. On average, each person saw the ad about 3.3 times.

These figures illustrate the arithmetic. They are not benchmarks or guaranteed delivery. Reach is a whole number of people; impressions and frequency can include fractions when averaged.

What counts as reach?

Each platform defines reach differently: some count unique devices, others unique logged-in users, and some estimate reach statistically rather than counting it exactly. Use the reach figure from your own ad platform's report, and compare frequency only between campaigns that measure reach the same way.

What is a good frequency?

There is no single correct frequency. Common planning ranges treat under 2x as low exposure, 2x to 5x as balanced for many campaigns, 5x to 10x as moderate to high, and 10x or more as a risk of ad fatigue or wasted spend. The right frequency depends on your creative, audience size, and campaign goal, so compare your own results over time rather than chasing one fixed number. For help judging a related metric, read What Is a Good CPM?

Reach and frequency vs CPM

CPM prices 1,000 impressions. Reach and frequency describe how those impressions were distributed: a high reach spreads impressions across more people at a lower frequency, while a low reach concentrates the same impressions on fewer people at a higher frequency. Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate frequency?

Divide total impressions by reach. For example, 500,000 impressions delivered to a reach of 150,000 people gives an average frequency of 3.3.

What is the difference between reach and impressions?

Reach counts each person once, no matter how many times they saw the ad. Impressions count every view, including repeats. Impressions divided by reach gives average frequency.

What is a good ad frequency?

It depends on the goal and channel. Many planners treat under 2x as low exposure, 2x to 5x as a balanced range for most campaigns, 5x to 10x as moderate to high, and 10x or more as a risk of ad fatigue. Treat these as starting points, not fixed rules, and compare against your own campaign history.

Can I calculate reach from frequency and impressions?

Yes. Divide impressions by frequency: reach = impressions ÷ frequency. For example, 500,000 impressions at a frequency of 3.3 gives a reach of about 151,500.
