What is CPV?

CPV (cost per view) is ad spend divided by the number of counted views. It shows the average amount you pay for each view of a video ad.

See what each video view costs you

CPV formula and example, with the formula shown below.

CPV = Ad spend ÷ Views

Worked example

A video campaign spends $1,500 and receives 50,000 views. CPV is $1,500 ÷ 50,000 = $0.03. At the same CPV, a $3,000 budget buys an estimated 100,000 views.

These figures illustrate the arithmetic. They are not market averages or guaranteed campaign results. Volume forecasts can contain fractions; actual views are whole counts.

What counts as a view?

Each platform defines a view differently, so use the views counted in your own ad report. On YouTube, Google Ads Help says a skippable in-stream view counts after 30 seconds, or at the end of a shorter ad, or when the viewer engages with the ad. In-feed and Shorts ads use different rules.

Because definitions differ, compare CPV only between campaigns that count views the same way. Reference: Google Ads Help: About video views.

How do CPV and CPM relate?

Both measure the cost of delivery. CPM prices 1,000 impressions. CPV prices each counted view. View rate, which is views divided by impressions, connects the two.

CPV = CPM ÷ (10 × view rate %)

You can also solve CPM = CPV × view rate % × 10. Enter view rate as a percentage: 25 means 25%.

Worked example

A campaign has a $10 CPM and a 25% view rate. That means 250 views per 1,000 impressions. CPV is $10 ÷ 250 = $0.04. This is an effective CPV based on your reported impressions and views, not a bid.

To compare impression costs, use our CPM calculator. To convert CPM using click-through rate instead, use the CPM to CPC calculator. For help judging a CPM, read What Is a Good CPM?

CPV and cost per completed view (CPCV)

CPV counts views under the platform's view rule. CPCV counts only views where the video played to the end. Calculate CPCV with the same formula, using completed views instead of all counted views. CPCV is usually higher than CPV for the same spend, because fewer views reach the end.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate CPV?

Divide ad spend by the number of counted views. For example, $1,500 spent on 50,000 views gives a CPV of $1,500 ÷ 50,000 = $0.03.

What is the difference between CPV and CPM?

CPM is the cost of 1,000 ad impressions. CPV is the cost of each counted view. To convert, use view rate: CPV = CPM ÷ (10 × view rate %). A $10 CPM with a 25% view rate gives a CPV of $0.04.

What is a good CPV?

There is no universal good CPV. Compare it with your own campaigns on the same platform, ad format, audience, and date range. A low CPV does not help if those views do not lead to the outcomes you want.

What is the difference between CPV and CPCV?

CPCV (cost per completed view) divides spend by views where the video played to the end. Use the same formula with completed views instead of all counted views.

Can I use organic or public view counts?

Use the paid views counted in your ad report for the same period. Organic views are not paid ad views. For influencer fees, divide the fee by the views you agreed to count, and note how those views are defined.

Can I use this calculator in any country?
