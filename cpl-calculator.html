What is CPL?

CPL (cost per lead) is ad spend divided by the number of leads. It shows the average amount you pay for each early-stage conversion, such as a form fill or signup.

CPL formula and example

CPL = Ad spend ÷ Leads

Worked example

A campaign spends $1,000 and generates 50 leads. CPL is $1,000 ÷ 50 = $20. At the same CPL, a $2,500 budget is expected to produce about 125 leads.

These figures illustrate the arithmetic. They are not market averages or guaranteed campaign results. Budget forecasts can contain fractions; actual leads are whole counts.

What counts as a lead?

A lead is whatever you define as an early-stage conversion: a form fill, a signed-up email, a demo request, or a content download. Lead quality varies a lot between sources, so a low CPL from one channel is not automatically better if those leads rarely become customers. Use the lead count from your own ad platform or CRM, and compare CPL only between campaigns that define a lead the same way.

CPL vs CPA vs CPC

These metrics price different stages of a funnel. CPC prices a single click. CPL prices a lead, an earlier-stage action like a form fill. CPA prices a completed acquisition, typically a sale or paying customer, and is usually the highest of the three for the same funnel.

To price a completed sale instead of a lead, use our CPA calculator. To compare click costs, use our CPC calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate CPL?

Divide ad spend by the number of leads. For example, $1,000 spent that produces 50 leads gives a CPL of $1,000 ÷ 50 = $20.

What counts as a lead?

A lead is any action you count as an early-stage conversion before a sale: a form fill, a signup, a demo request, or a download. Platforms track this differently, so use the lead count from your own ad platform or CRM report.

What is the difference between CPL and CPA?

CPL (cost per lead) prices an earlier-stage action, like a form fill. CPA (cost per acquisition) prices a completed sale or paying customer, further down the funnel. CPA is typically higher than CPL because not every lead converts into a sale.

What is a good CPL?

There is no single good CPL. A good CPL is one that, combined with your lead-to-customer conversion rate, keeps your CPA at or below what a customer is worth to you. Compare your CPL against your own past campaigns on the same platform and goal rather than a generic benchmark.
